## The prompt

Attach the survivor's photo first and the photo of the one who turns second, then paste:

```text
Emotional 12-second post-apocalyptic short film, vertical 9:16, cinematic and realistic.
The person in image 1 is the survivor; the person in image 2 has turned into a zombie
with pale grey skin and clouded white eyes but is still clearly recognizable.
Beat 1: close-up of the survivor raising a pistol with shaking hands, tears on their face,
on a dark ruined street with smoke.
Beat 2: the zombie staggers closer, then stops and tilts its head, recognizing them.
Beat 3: the survivor lowers the gun; the zombie gently hugs them instead of biting,
and the survivor hugs back, crying.
Beat 4: hard cut to a warm sunny flashback of the same two, both healthy and human,
laughing together on a picnic blanket in a green park. Golden light, film grain, slow motion.
Keep both faces consistent. No blood, no gore, no text. Soft emotional piano score, no dialogue.
```

## Change only the flashback

The last beat is what makes the clip yours. Swap the picnic for one of these, or your own:

- walking barefoot hand in hand on a beach at golden sunset
- laughing and hugging on a bright snowy mountain slope
- cooking noodles together in a cozy warm kitchen
- dancing together on their wedding day under string lights
- for a dog: playing fetch in a sunny park; for a cat: curled up asleep in a sunny window

Keep the other lines as they are. They fix who is who, keep the scene non-graphic and keep the faces stable.

## Photo tips

- **Clear, front-facing faces.** Even light, the whole head in frame, no sunglasses or heavy filters.
- **One person per photo.** Crop group shots down to one face.
- **Right order.** Image 1 holds the gun. Image 2 turns, and is the healthy one in the flashback. Swapping them is the most common reason a clip feels wrong.
- **Pets:** shoot at eye level with both eyes visible. Clear markings help the model keep them recognisable.

## Mistakes that ruin most clips

- **Asking for blood or a bite.** Most models refuse or blur it, and the clip loses the tenderness that makes it work.
- **Two separate generations.** Making the zombie scene and the flashback separately is where faces drift. One continuous clip keeps them consistent.
- **Too short.** Under eight seconds, one of the four beats gets cut. Twelve seconds fits all four; fifteen gives the memory more room.
